WCFFBP Methodology
Purpose
The Washington County Federal Fiscal Balance Project (WCFFBP) measures the fiscal relationship between Washington State and the federal government.
The project seeks to estimate, by year and ultimately by county:
- federal dollars flowing into Washington;
- federal dollars flowing out of Washington; and
- the resulting federal fiscal balance.
The governing reporting convention is:
Federal Fiscal Balance = Federal Inflows to Washington − Federal Outflows from Washington
A positive balance means Washington received more from the federal government than it sent.
A negative balance means Washington sent more to the federal government than it received.
The same convention applies to county-level balances.
WCFFBP is designed as an open, reproducible research system. Its objective is not to produce a particular answer. Its objective is to make the answer progressively harder to dispute.
Methodological Standard
WCFFBP follows a simple professional standard:
An independent researcher using the same source data and documented methodology should be able to obtain substantially the same result.

If independent replication produces a materially different result, the discrepancy should be investigated rather than suppressed. The methodology, implementation, source interpretation, or documentation may require improvement.
WCFFBP therefore treats methodology as a versioned research product rather than as a permanently settled conclusion.
Measure First
WCFFBP separates measurement from interpretation.
The project attempts first to determine:
1. what a federal source actually reports;
2. what accounting measure the reported value represents;
3. what time period the value describes;
4. what geography the value describes;
5. whether the value overlaps another federal reporting system;
6. whether it represents a distinct economic flow; and
7. whether sufficient evidence exists to include it in the fiscal-balance ledger.
Policy interpretation follows measurement rather than determining it.
Measure first. Explain later.

Federal Inflows and Federal Outflows
Federal Inflows
Federal inflows are federal expenditures attributable to Washington State or to a Washington county under the applicable WCFFBP accounting and geographic-incidence methodology.
Potential inflow categories include, among others:
- direct payments to individuals;
- Social Security;
- Medicare;
- Medicaid and CHIP;
- veterans expenditures;
- federal assistance;
- procurement;
- federal civilian compensation;
- military compensation;
- retirement payments;
- housing assistance;
- unemployment insurance;
- transportation expenditures; and
- other federal programs for which defensible Washington attribution can be established.
The existence of a Washington-associated federal record does not automatically make the associated dollar amount a Washington federal inflow.
Federal Outflows
Federal outflows are federal taxes and other federal receipts attributable to Washington under the applicable WCFFBP receipts methodology.
Federal administrative collection geography and ultimate economic incidence are not assumed to be identical.
Where direct Washington observations are available, they are preserved. Where an allocation model is necessary, the allocator and its assumptions must be explicit and reproducible.
Source Authority, Accounting Authority, and Geographic Authority
WCFFBP distinguishes three questions that are sometimes incorrectly treated as one:
Source authority: Is the underlying observation obtained from an authoritative source?
Accounting authority: Is that source the appropriate accounting path for the economic flow being measured?
Geographic-incidence authority: Does the reported geography establish where the economic benefit, burden, payment, or activity should be attributed?
An authoritative federal dataset can answer the first question without necessarily answering the second or third.
Accordingly, a federal-agency citation alone does not establish ledger inclusion or geographic incidence.
Evidentiary Chain
WCFFBP seeks to preserve a reproducible chain from every material published result to its authoritative source.
Depending on the component, that chain may include:
published result → ledger/component record → accounting policy → reconciliation or validation evidence → transformed observation → acquisition record → authoritative source
Not every source currently contains every element of this chain.
Where a link is incomplete, WCFFBP seeks to disclose the limitation rather than infer the missing evidence.
Measurement Is Not Accounting Treatment
WCFFBP deliberately separates:
- source acquisition;
- measurement and validation;
- semantic interpretation;
- overlap and reconciliation analysis;
- accounting-policy decisions;
- ledger inclusion; and
- geographic incidence or allocation.
Successful acquisition does not imply successful validation.
Successful validation does not imply ledger inclusion.
Ledger inclusion at the state level does not establish county-level incidence.
A source may therefore be valid, important, and extensively preserved in WCFFBP while contributing no additive dollars to a particular published ledger.
One Federal Dollar, One Accounting Path
Federal expenditures frequently appear in more than one federal reporting system.
WCFFBP therefore applies a no-double-counting invariant:
The same underlying federal economic resource may enter the principal fiscal-balance ledger only once.

When two sources appear to represent the same underlying economic flow, WCFFBP identifies a preferred accounting source when sufficient evidence exists.
The overlapping source remains preserved for provenance, reconciliation, transaction-level analysis, or geographic investigation, but it does not create a second additive accounting path.
This principle applies regardless of whether the overlapping values are numerically identical. Different federal systems may record obligations, expenditures, payments, awards, or other accounting measures at different times.
Economic overlap therefore does not require dollar-for-dollar equality between reporting systems.
Affirmative Evidence Standard
WCFFBP does not assume that a federal expenditure is additive merely because it appears in a different dataset.
It also does not assume that a federal expenditure is duplicative merely because its program name resembles another accounting stream.
The governing rule is:
Classification requires affirmative evidence.

When evidence establishes that a stream is economically distinct from existing ledger components, it may be included.
When evidence establishes that the stream represents an economic flow already captured through a preferred source, it is excluded from the additional accounting path while remaining preserved as evidence.
When neither proposition can be established, the amount remains unresolved.
Uncertainty is not resolved by choosing whichever treatment produces the larger or smaller fiscal balance.
Assistance Accounting Resolution
USAspending assistance requires particular care because one transaction system can contain programs that are also represented in specialized agency accounting systems.
WCFFBP resolves assistance transactions using the following hierarchy:
1. Award-specific accounting classification
2. Explicit program-to-accounting-policy mapping
3. Unclassified review
Award-specific evidence takes precedence over program-level policy.
Accounting treatment is not inferred from:
- broad agency identity;
- Assistance Listing prefixes;
- fuzzy program-name similarity;
- broad program families; or
- the mere existence of a Washington recipient.
This structure permits two awards reported under the same Assistance Listing to receive different accounting treatments when the evidence demonstrates that they represent economically different streams.
Accounting Dispositions
Depending on the analytical layer, WCFFBP uses dispositions including:
INCLUDE / INCLUDE_ADDITIVE
Evidence supports treatment as a distinct federal inflow under the applicable methodology.
EXCLUDE_DUPLICATE
Evidence supports the conclusion that the economic stream is already represented through another preferred accounting path.
WITHHOLD_UNRESOLVED / REVIEW_REQUIRED
Evidence is insufficient to establish either additive treatment or duplication.
REFERENCE_ONLY
The observation is retained for comparison, provenance, reconciliation, or other analytical purposes but is not an additive ledger component.
UNCLASSIFIED_REVIEW
No approved accounting policy yet resolves the observation.
These categories describe accounting treatment. They do not erase or modify the underlying source observation.
Preferred Sources
WCFFBP does not assume that one federal reporting system is universally superior to another.
Preferred sources are selected by economic component.
A specialized program source may be preferred over USAspending for one component, while USAspending may be the appropriate source for another economically distinct component.
Examples already established in the WCFFBP methodology include specialized accounting treatment for Social Security, veterans expenditures, Medicare, federal wages, and multiple federal assistance programs.
Preferred-source decisions are versioned and supported by reconciliation evidence.
Time
Federal datasets do not all report on the same time basis.
Sources may represent:
- federal fiscal years;
- calendar years;
- tax years;
- monthly observations;
- point-in-time observations;
- obligations;
- expenditures;
- payments; or
- annualized run rates.
WCFFBP preserves the native time basis of the source.
A value is not silently converted to a federal fiscal-year amount merely because the final analytical objective is a federal fiscal-year balance.
Where a transformation is authorized, the transformation must be explicit and reproducible.
Where no defensible transformation has been established, the timing limitation remains visible.
Geography and Incidence
Federal datasets contain several geographic concepts that are not economically interchangeable.
These may include:
- taxpayer location;
- recipient location;
- place of performance;
- beneficiary residence;
- agency location;
- provider location;
- award location;
- administrative reporting geography; and
- economic incidence.
WCFFBP does not automatically treat one geographic field as evidence of another.
In particular:
A Washington-associated administrative record does not by itself establish Washington economic incidence.

County allocation requires its own evidentiary basis.
State-level accounting authority therefore does not automatically authorize county-level allocation.
Observed, Allocated, Estimated, and Unallocable Values
WCFFBP seeks to distinguish among:
Observed — directly reported for the relevant geography and accounting concept.
Allocated — derived from a documented geographic allocator.
Estimated — derived from an explicit analytical model where direct observation or a standard allocator is unavailable.
Unallocable — retained for reconciliation or completeness but not assigned to Washington or its counties without defensible evidence.
These categories should not be silently combined or relabeled.
Missing, Suppressed, and Unresolved Values
WCFFBP does not treat the following as equivalent:
- zero;
- missing;
- suppressed;
- unavailable;
- not applicable;
- unresolved;
- not yet acquired; and
- outside the valid period of an accounting policy.
In particular:
Suppressed is not zero.

Missing values are not automatically imputed.
An unresolved stream is not automatically excluded permanently; it remains a research question until sufficient evidence supports an accounting disposition.
No Unsupported Extrapolation
WCFFBP does not fill known data gaps merely to create a complete-looking time series.
Partial-year observations are not silently annualized.
A policy established for FY2020–FY2024 does not automatically authorize values for FY2015–FY2019 or FY2025.
A missing year is not automatically interpolated from neighboring years.
When a material component remains incomplete, the limitation is disclosed with the result.
A provisional fiscal balance may therefore be published as incomplete rather than converted into an apparently precise but unsupported final estimate.
COVID and Extraordinary Funding
COVID-related spending is preserved as an independent analytical characteristic wherever it can be identified.
Accounting disposition and COVID classification answer different questions.
A transaction may be economically duplicative and still retain important COVID provenance.
Likewise, identification as COVID-related does not by itself determine whether the transaction belongs in or outside the principal ledger.
WCFFBP therefore seeks to support both inclusive and COVID-excluded analytical views without changing the underlying accounting classification.
Other extraordinary federal funding streams may be treated similarly.
Validation and Reconciliation
Validation occurs throughout the analytical process rather than only after a final result has been produced.
Depending on the subsystem, validation may include:
- source-file integrity;
- row-count reconciliation;
- schema validation;
- identity consistency;
- duplicate detection;
- referential integrity;
- fiscal-year coverage;
- geographic coverage;
- accounting bridge closure;
- control-total reconciliation;
- classification coverage;
- deterministic rerun behavior; and
- explicit PASS/HOLD assertions.
Where an accounting universe is partitioned into included, excluded, unresolved, or other classifications, WCFFBP seeks to require the classifications to reconcile back to the complete observed universe.
A residual difference should be explained rather than silently discarded.
Deterministic Processing
WCFFBP seeks deterministic processing wherever practicable.
Identical source data, methodology, and code should produce identical transformed data and analytical results.
Raw source observations are not manually edited to make them conform to an expected result.
Corrections, classifications, crosswalks, and accounting decisions are implemented through documented transformations or reference structures so that the original observation remains recoverable.
Fail-Closed Accounting
When a required classification or accounting relationship cannot be established, WCFFBP generally favors a fail-closed approach.
An unresolved dollar does not receive an additive plus sign merely because no duplicate has yet been identified.
Likewise, it is not discarded merely because duplication appears plausible.
The unresolved amount remains visible in the research record until affirmative evidence supports a treatment.
This approach intentionally favors defensibility over apparent completeness.
Historical Evidence and Methodological Change
WCFFBP preserves historical analytical artifacts even when later evidence changes the project's preferred treatment.
Older diagnostics may therefore contain:
- superseded source preferences;
- preliminary accounting classifications;
- earlier sign conventions;
- unresolved questions later answered;
- provisional values; or
- analytical approaches no longer used in the current reference calculation.
These artifacts remain part of the audit trail.
The current methodology is determined by the applicable versioned accounting policy and publication methodology, not merely by the oldest surviving research artifact.
Historical evidence is preserved; superseded policy is not silently treated as current policy.
Sign Convention and Historical Artifacts
The canonical WCFFBP public reporting convention is:
Federal Fiscal Balance = Federal Inflows to Washington − Federal Outflows from Washington
Some historical diagnostic artifacts were constructed using the opposite arithmetic, such as:
Federal Receipts − Federal Inflows
Those historical artifacts are not rewritten because doing so would alter the research record.
Where such evidence contributes to a current publication, the transformation to the canonical Washington-perspective sign convention must be explicit.
External Comparisons
External balance-of-payments studies, including work by the Rockefeller Institute of Government, are valuable methodological references and external reconciliation checks.
They are not WCFFBP calibration targets.
WCFFBP does not alter an independently constructed Washington value merely to reproduce an external published result.
A material difference should instead trigger investigation of:
- source selection;
- accounting definitions;
- allocation methodology;
- time period;
- geographic incidence;
- coverage; and
- other methodological differences.
Agreement provides useful corroboration.
Disagreement provides useful diagnostic evidence.
Neither determines the result by itself.
No Predetermined Result
WCFFBP does not select sources or accounting treatments according to whether they make Washington's fiscal balance more positive or more negative.
The same evidentiary standard applies in both directions.
A newly identified federal expenditure should be included when the evidence supports inclusion even if it makes Washington appear more favorable in the federal fiscal relationship.
A newly identified receipt or corrected expenditure should likewise be incorporated when supported even if it makes Washington appear less favorable.
The direction of the result is not a methodological criterion.
DRAFT Results
A WCFFBP DRAFT result represents the best reproducible accounting state supported by the methodology at that version of the project.
It does not imply that every federal flow has been identified or that every geographic-incidence question has been resolved.
Known material omissions, unresolved streams, partial-year sources, provisional treatments, and other limitations must accompany the result.
A DRAFT result may change when better evidence becomes available.
Such change is expected behavior in a versioned research project, not evidence that earlier uncertainty should have been concealed.
Reproducibility
A published WCFFBP analytical result should identify, where applicable:
- repository version;
- Git commit;
- methodology version;
- source datasets;
- acquisition records;
- load batches;
- accounting policies;
- validation evidence;
- evidence packages; and
- known limitations.
The goal is not merely to publish a number.
The goal is to make it possible for another researcher to determine where the number came from, reproduce it, challenge it, and propose a better-supported treatment.
Reference Methodology
WCFFBP maintains a versioned reference methodology and reference calculation.
Independent replication, criticism, modification, and methodological improvement are encouraged.
A derivative analysis may reach a different result.
Where it materially modifies WCFFBP methodology, however, it should be described as based on or derived from WCFFBP rather than represented as the official WCFFBP Reference Calculation.
The project owns its reference methodology.
It does not own the question.
Continuing Methodological Development
WCFFBP remains an active research project.
Important questions remain open, including additional federal expenditure streams, federal receipts incidence, common-period presentation, incomplete source years, and county-level allocation for sources whose state-level accounting treatment has already been established.
Open questions are retained explicitly rather than resolved through unsupported assumptions.
The methodology will therefore continue to evolve as new evidence, better federal data, improved reconciliation, and independent review become available.
The objective is not to make the methodology unchangeable.
The objective is to make every change visible, evidence-based, reproducible, and reviewable.
Measure first. Explain later.
