VA procurement → GDX reconciliation framework - NOTES

1. Initial question: whether FY2024 USAspending VA procurement of $384.771M was additive to the $6.927B GDX control.

2. Accounting-basis discovery: USAspending procurement represents obligations, while GDX represents expenditures/payments; Tahoma's $73.086M FY2024 obligation for performance beginning in FY2025 was the decisive example.

3. Timing evidence: Query 141 identified $76.606M of positive obligations associated with awards whose performance begins after FY2024 and −$32.263M of negative modifications.

4. Scope evidence: Query 143 classified $322.261M, or 83.75%, with high confidence into Medical Care, Construction, and Memorial Benefits candidate activity families.

5. Residual investigation: Queries 144–145 showed why PSC classification alone cannot establish duplication and revealed such things as Veteran transportation, enterprise telecommunications, administrative acquisition support, and mixed-purpose services.

6. Geographic discovery: USAspending's WA place of performance cannot automatically be interpreted as Washington-only VA expenditure. We found enterprise contracts and transactions explicitly benefiting out-of-state VA facilities.

7. Geographic control: Query 148 conservatively identified $152.088M as confirmed Washington-local, $10.927M as enterprise/multistate, $12.589M as benefiting identified non-Washington facilities, and left $209.167M unresolved rather than manufacturing attribution.

8. Authoritative-source conclusion: VA's revised GDX methodology supports GDX as the expenditure control and explicitly encompasses the major institutional expenditure families implicated by procurement.

9. Resulting policy: USAspending VA procurement is non-additive to GDX by default. Preserve every USAspending transaction for reconciliation/audit purposes. Separately include a procurement class only when affirmative evidence demonstrates that the expenditure is outside GDX scope.

10. Critical qualification: EXCLUDE_DUPLICATE does not mean we proved a one-for-one correspondence between $384.771M of FY2024 obligations and $384.771M contained within FY2024 GDX. It means adding the obligation stream to the expenditure control would be methodologically invalid and create an unacceptable double-counting risk.

That tenth point is particularly worth preserving because somebody reviewing WCFFBP years from now could otherwise misunderstand what EXCLUDE_DUPLICATE means.
