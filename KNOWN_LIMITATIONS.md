# WCFFBP Known Limitations

## Washington County Federal Fiscal Balance Project

The Washington County Federal Fiscal Balance Project (WCFFBP) is an open, versioned research project.

Its purpose is to construct a transparent and reproducible accounting of federal dollars flowing into and out of Washington State and, ultimately, each of Washington's 39 counties.

The project is intentionally conservative about what its evidence can establish.

A source may be authoritative for a dollar amount without establishing its accounting treatment. A statewide accounting control may be defensible without establishing county economic incidence. A federal record may be observed without being authorized for additive treatment in the fiscal-balance ledger.

For these reasons, WCFFBP preserves limitations rather than resolving them through unsupported assumptions.

> **A known limitation is not necessarily an error. A limitation identifies the boundary beyond which the current evidence or methodology does not support a stronger claim.**

Likewise:

> **WCFFBP does not resolve uncertainty by assigning an unsupported value. Where evidence is insufficient, the limitation remains visible in the research record.**

This document describes the principal known limitations of the current WCFFBP DRAFT-1 research state.

---

## 1. DRAFT-1 Is an Incomplete Research State

DRAFT-1 is not presented as the final Washington federal fiscal balance.

The project has established substantial portions of the federal receipts and federal expenditure framework, but additional federal flows, accounting questions, time-basis questions, and geographic-incidence questions remain open.

Accordingly, a DRAFT-1 fiscal balance should be interpreted as the balance produced by the components presently authorized under the applicable WCFFBP methodology, together with any explicitly identified provisional treatments.

It should not be interpreted as evidence that every federal inflow or outflow has been identified.

The existence of a calculated balance does not convert an incomplete accounting universe into a complete one.

---

## 2. Known Omission, Known Uncertainty, and Scope Boundary Are Different

WCFFBP distinguishes three broad kinds of limitation.

### Known omission

A component is known or strongly expected to belong within the eventual accounting universe, but a sufficiently supported value has not yet been incorporated.

### Known uncertainty

Relevant observations exist, but the evidence does not yet establish some required element of treatment, such as:

- accounting additivity;
- overlap;
- timing;
- completeness;
- geographic attribution;
- economic incidence; or
- allocation methodology.

### Scope boundary

The question lies outside what the WCFFBP fiscal-balance calculation itself is designed to establish.

These categories should not be treated as interchangeable.

In particular, an unresolved component is not automatically an omitted expenditure, and an omitted component is not automatically evidence of a methodological error.

---

## 3. The Federal Expenditure Universe Is Not Yet Complete

WCFFBP has identified and incorporated substantial federal expenditure streams, but DRAFT-1 does not claim that the expenditure side of the ledger is complete.

Additional expenditure components remain under investigation or require further reconciliation before inclusion.

A known federal program does not automatically receive an additive plus sign merely because federal spending occurred.

Before inclusion, WCFFBP may need to establish:

1. what the source measures;
2. whether the amount is complete;
3. whether it overlaps another accounting source;
4. what time period it represents;
5. whether the amount is attributable to Washington; and
6. whether sufficient evidence exists for statewide or county-level treatment.

Where those questions remain unresolved, the component may remain outside the principal reference calculation.

This can make a provisional DRAFT-1 inflow total lower than a later, more complete reference calculation.

It does not follow, however, that every unresolved amount will ultimately be additive. Some unresolved observations may later be determined to overlap amounts already represented elsewhere.

---

## 4. Some Assistance Accounting Remains Provisional or Unresolved

The USAspending assistance domain contains a large and heterogeneous population of awards and transactions.

WCFFBP has developed explicit accounting controls to distinguish:

- additive assistance;
- duplicate or overlapping assistance;
- provisional extraordinary funding;
- allocation or reference observations;
- withheld awards or programs; and
- unresolved ordinary assistance.

DRAFT-1 evidence includes explicitly provisional treatment of identified COVID, Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA), and other extraordinary transactions.

The DRAFT-1 assistance bridge also preserves unresolved and withheld populations rather than assigning them unsupported additive treatment.

Accordingly, the DRAFT-1 assistance total should not be interpreted as a final classification of every USAspending assistance transaction associated with Washington.

Future evidence may move individual awards or programs among accounting dispositions.

Any such change should be documented as a methodological revision rather than silently incorporated into the historical DRAFT-1 result.

---

## 5. Some Medicare-Related Components Remain Under Investigation

WCFFBP maintains substantial CMS evidence, but the Medicare-related accounting universe is not uniformly resolved across all program components.

Different Medicare sources may describe different concepts, including:

- fee-for-service expenditures;
- managed-care or Part C payments;
- prescription-drug-related expenditures;
- subsidy programs;
- beneficiary geography;
- provider geography;
- accountable-care arrangements; and
- shared-savings or loss measures.

These concepts are not automatically additive.

For example, WCFFBP has identified the Retiree Drug Subsidy as a potentially distinct federal expenditure stream, but available evidence has not yet established sufficient dollar incidence for ledger inclusion.

Likewise, validation of Medicare Shared Savings Program data does not itself establish that shared-savings or loss amounts can be allocated among Washington counties.

Partial-year Medicare observations are not silently annualized.

The existence of CMS data therefore does not imply that every Medicare-related observation has been authorized for statewide or county ledger treatment.

---

## 6. Source Suppression Limits Some Measurements

Some federal datasets suppress or obscure observations for confidentiality, disclosure-control, or other source-defined reasons.

Suppression is not equivalent to zero.

WCFFBP therefore does not replace suppressed observations with zero merely to make a calculation complete.

This issue is material in some source domains.

For example, OPM FedScope salary data exhibit source suppression that must be considered when using employee salary observations as evidence for federal civilian compensation allocation.

CMS datasets may also contain suppressed observations.

Where suppression prevents a complete direct measurement, WCFFBP must either:

- establish an explicit and defensible methodology for handling the suppression;
- use the source for a more limited purpose; or
- preserve the limitation.

The project does not assume that the unobserved amount is zero.

---

## 7. Time Bases Are Heterogeneous

WCFFBP integrates federal data reported on different time bases.

Depending on the source, an observation may represent:

- federal fiscal year;
- calendar year;
- tax year;
- report year;
- monthly period;
- quarterly period;
- point-in-time observation; or
- partial-year period.

These concepts are not automatically interchangeable.

A calendar-year observation does not become a federal-fiscal-year observation merely because both carry the same year number.

Similarly, a partial-year value is not a complete annual value.

Where WCFFBP establishes an explicit temporal conversion, annualization, or allocation method, that transformation should be reproducible and identified.

Where no defensible common-period transformation has been established, the native time basis remains a limitation on direct comparison.

DRAFT-1 therefore contains some components whose common-period presentation remains an active methodological question.

---

## 8. Administrative Geography Is Not Economic Incidence

Federal datasets frequently report geography for administrative purposes.

Depending on the source, geography may describe:

- taxpayer filing location;
- recipient address;
- award recipient location;
- place of performance;
- beneficiary residence;
- provider location;
- employer location;
- duty station;
- administrative office; or
- another source-specific concept.

These geographic concepts are not interchangeable.

A federal record associated administratively with Washington does not necessarily establish that the underlying economic benefit or burden belongs entirely to Washington.

Likewise, a Washington county appearing in a federal source does not necessarily establish county economic incidence.

WCFFBP therefore distinguishes:

**source geography → accounting geography → economic incidence**

where the evidence requires those concepts to be separated.

This distinction can limit both statewide attribution and county allocation.

---

## 9. Statewide Accounting Does Not Automatically Produce County Balances

The ultimate objective of WCFFBP is an annual federal fiscal balance for each of Washington's 39 counties.

DRAFT-1 does not yet achieve that objective for every component.

Some sources provide defensible statewide accounting controls but do not provide a defensible county allocation.

Others provide county-level administrative observations that may not represent the economic-incidence concept required by WCFFBP.

Accordingly:

> **Statewide accounting authority does not automatically confer county-incidence authority.**

County allocation requires its own evidentiary basis.

A component may therefore be:

- included in the Washington statewide ledger;
- preserved in the database;
- fully validated as a source observation; and
- still withheld from county allocation.

This is an intentional methodological constraint rather than a database failure.

---

## 10. Federal Receipts Attribution Has Incidence Limitations

Federal receipts attributable to Washington can be measured using administrative tax and receipt data, but administrative collection geography is not necessarily identical to ultimate economic incidence.

For example, a tax reported by the IRS as associated with Washington is appropriately described as an **IRS-reported tax attributed to Washington** where that is what the source establishes.

That description does not, by itself, establish who ultimately bears the economic burden of the tax.

This distinction is particularly important for taxes whose economic incidence may differ from the administrative location of collection or reporting.

WCFFBP therefore distinguishes administrative attribution from modeled economic incidence.

Where the project uses administrative receipts data, the resulting fiscal-balance calculation should be interpreted according to that measurement concept unless a separate incidence methodology has been established.

---

## 11. Overlapping Federal Reporting Systems Create Accounting Risk

Federal expenditures can appear in more than one federal reporting system.

For example, the same underlying economic resource may be represented through:

- agency expenditure reports;
- benefit-payment data;
- USAspending assistance;
- USAspending procurement;
- program-specific federal reports;
- financial control totals; or
- transaction-level administrative systems.

These observations may differ in:

- amount;
- timing;
- transaction grain;
- accounting basis;
- administrative identifier; and
- geographic representation.

Numerical differences do not prove that two sources describe different economic resources.

Likewise, numerical agreement does not by itself prove that two observations are duplicates.

WCFFBP therefore requires reconciliation before potentially overlapping sources are combined.

This reduces double-counting risk but creates a corresponding limitation: some observed federal dollars may remain unresolved until their relationship to other sources is sufficiently established.

---

## 12. Missing, Suppressed, Partial, Unresolved, and Withheld Are Not Zero

WCFFBP intentionally preserves distinctions among:

- observed zero;
- missing;
- suppressed;
- unavailable;
- partial;
- incomplete;
- unresolved;
- withheld; and
- not applicable.

These states have different meanings.

An observed zero is evidence of zero under the applicable source definition.

A missing value is not.

A suppressed value is not.

A partial observation is not a complete observation.

An unresolved amount is not an authorized additive amount.

A withheld amount is intentionally prevented from entering the applicable ledger pending resolution.

Consequently, WCFFBP may publish a result with visible incompleteness rather than substitute an unsupported zero or estimate.

This can make a DRAFT-1 result appear less complete than an analysis that fills every cell.

That difference is intentional.

---

## 13. Historical Analytical Artifacts May Not Represent Current Methodology

WCFFBP preserves historical analytical artifacts as part of the audit trail.

Those artifacts may contain:

- earlier accounting classifications;
- provisional calculations;
- unresolved questions later answered;
- earlier source interpretations;
- superseded analytical views; or
- an earlier sign convention.

Their preservation is intentional.

They document how the research developed.

They should not automatically be interpreted as the current WCFFBP Reference Calculation.

In particular, some historical fiscal-balance diagnostics used arithmetic opposite to the current public sign convention.

The current convention is:

**Federal Fiscal Balance = Federal Inflows to Washington − Federal Outflows from Washington**

Historical evidence is not rewritten merely to make it appear as though the current convention had always been used.

---

## 14. DRAFT-1 Reproducibility Is Substantial but Not Yet Fully Automated

WCFFBP contains source acquisition code, loaders, transformations, migrations, validation routines, reconciliation evidence, accounting-policy structures, and published methodology.

However, DRAFT-1 does not yet provide a single command that reconstructs every historical source acquisition, database state, accounting decision, and published result from an empty environment.

Some source workflows remain source-specific.

Some upstream files are not stored in Git and must be reacquired.

Some historical publications may change or disappear.

Some third-party source material is cited rather than redistributed.

These issues are described more fully in `REPRODUCIBILITY.md`.

They are limitations of the present reproduction infrastructure, not evidence that the analytical result should be treated as irreproducible.

The relevant standard is whether an independent researcher can trace the evidence, understand the transformations and accounting decisions, reconstruct the applicable calculation, and identify the cause of material differences.

---

## 15. DRAFT-1 Is Not a Measure of All Economic Benefits and Burdens

WCFFBP measures a defined fiscal relationship.

Under the current public convention:

**Federal Fiscal Balance = Federal Inflows to Washington − Federal Outflows from Washington**

That accounting identity should not be interpreted as answering every economic or policy question concerning Washington's relationship with the federal government.

By itself, a WCFFBP fiscal balance does not establish:

- the total economic value Washington residents receive from federal activity;
- the ultimate economic incidence of every federal tax;
- the distribution of benefits or burdens among individuals or households;
- the value of national public goods;
- the value to Washington of federal activity occurring outside Washington;
- the economic multiplier or secondary effects of federal spending;
- the counterfactual economic condition that would exist under different federal policy;
- whether a particular federal program is efficient;
- whether a positive fiscal balance is economically desirable;
- whether a negative fiscal balance is economically undesirable; or
- whether the federal-state relationship is politically or normatively "fair."

Those are separate analytical questions.

WCFFBP may provide evidence relevant to some of them, but the fiscal-balance calculation alone does not resolve them.

This distinction is important in both directions.

A negative Washington fiscal balance should not automatically be characterized as proof of unfair treatment.

A positive Washington fiscal balance should not automatically be characterized as proof that Washington receives adequate value from the federal government.

WCFFBP measures the fiscal balance defined by its methodology.

Interpretation beyond that measurement requires additional evidence.

---

## 16. Limitations Can Change

This document describes known limitations of the current DRAFT-1 research state.

It is not intended to become a permanent list of deficiencies.

A limitation may later be:

- resolved through better source data;
- resolved through stronger reconciliation;
- replaced by a superior accounting source;
- reduced through improved allocation;
- quantified through sensitivity analysis;
- converted into an explicit methodological rule; or
- shown not to affect the reference calculation.

Conversely, new research may identify limitations that are not currently recognized.

WCFFBP therefore treats the limitations record as versioned research.

When a material limitation is resolved, the project should preserve enough historical evidence to determine:

1. what the limitation was;
2. what evidence resolved it;
3. what methodological change resulted; and
4. what numerical effect the change had, where applicable.

---

## 17. A Limitation Is Not a License to Guess

The presence of known limitations does not justify filling gaps with values selected to make the accounting appear complete.

WCFFBP does not seek completeness at the expense of evidentiary support.

Where a material value remains unknown, unresolved, suppressed, incomplete, or geographically unallocable, the preferred response is to preserve that condition explicitly until a defensible method is established.

This means that some DRAFT-1 totals may be incomplete.

It also means that those totals should be more interpretable than totals whose apparent completeness depends on undocumented assumptions.

The objective is not to eliminate uncertainty from the presentation.

The objective is to make uncertainty **visible, bounded where possible, and reproducible**.

---

## 18. How to Read a DRAFT-1 Result

A DRAFT-1 result should therefore be read as:

> **The federal fiscal balance supported by the sources, accounting policies, reconciliations, time treatments, and geographic-incidence decisions authorized for that WCFFBP research state, subject to the limitations explicitly identified with the result.**

It should not be read as:

> **A claim that every federal dollar affecting Washington has been discovered, perfectly measured, assigned to the correct period, attributed to its ultimate economic incidence, and allocated among all 39 counties without uncertainty.**

The distinction is fundamental.

WCFFBP publishes a versioned measurement under explicit rules.

As the evidence improves, the measurement may change.

A changed result supported by stronger evidence is not a failure of the research process.

It is the purpose of the research process.

---

## 19. Relationship to the WCFFBP Reference Calculation

The **WCFFBP Reference Calculation** is the result produced under the project's current adopted reference methodology for the applicable version.

The existence of known limitations does not eliminate the usefulness of a reference calculation.

It defines the conditions under which that calculation should be interpreted.

Researchers may propose alternative:

- sources;
- accounting treatments;
- incidence models;
- temporal transformations;
- allocation methods; or
- interpretations.

Those alternatives are welcome when they are explicit and reproducible.

A materially modified calculation, however, should be identified as a derivative or alternative calculation unless and until the underlying methodological change is adopted into the WCFFBP reference methodology.

The project owns its reference methodology.

It does not own the question.

---

## 20. The Standard for Disclosure

WCFFBP should disclose a limitation when it could materially affect a reasonable reader's interpretation of:

- the completeness of the accounting universe;
- the numerical fiscal balance;
- the comparability of periods;
- the treatment of overlapping sources;
- the attribution of dollars to Washington;
- the allocation of dollars among counties; or
- the meaning of the resulting measure.

Minor technical imperfections that do not materially affect interpretation need not be elevated into headline methodological limitations.

Conversely, a material limitation should not be buried merely because resolving it would be inconvenient.

The appropriate response to a known limitation is:

**identify it → preserve it → quantify it where defensible → investigate it → resolve it when evidence permits → document the change**

That process is part of the WCFFBP methodology.

---

## 21. Current Research Boundary

At DRAFT-1, the principal research boundary can be stated simply:

WCFFBP has progressed far enough to construct and audit substantial components of Washington's federal fiscal relationship, but not far enough to claim a complete and final annual federal fiscal balance across all source families and all 39 counties.

The remaining work includes, among other matters:

- additional federal expenditure streams;
- unresolved or provisional accounting classifications;
- incomplete source periods;
- common-period treatment for heterogeneous time bases;
- federal-receipts incidence questions;
- suppression-sensitive source domains;
- unresolved Medicare-related components;
- continued overlap reconciliation; and
- county-level allocation for sources whose statewide accounting treatment is stronger than their county-incidence evidence.

These limitations should accompany the applicable published results.

They should not be used to conceal those results.

Nor should the existence of a provisional result be used to conceal its limitations.

Both belong in the research record.

---

## 22. The Limitation Standard

The purpose of `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md` is not to weaken the WCFFBP calculation.

It is to define precisely where the calculation's evidentiary authority ends.

A transparent research project should be able to say both:

> **This is what the evidence supports.**

and:

> **This is what the evidence does not yet support.**

WCFFBP treats both statements as results.

The objective is not to produce a particular answer.

It is to make the answer progressively harder to dispute.

**Measure first. Explain later.**
